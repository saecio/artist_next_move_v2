## DECIDED

- Master is the current Work proxy.
- Temporal snapshots are mandatory.
- Evaluation is out-of-time.
- Historical baselines come before ML.
- Time Machine uses historical out-of-time predictions.

Added the missing masters_df["year"] fix in the notebook and re-ran sections 7 to 9 on 2026-10-06.

- Works tagged reissue/repress/remaster (via format_descriptions) that share artist_id + normalized title with an earlier-year work by the same artist are removed, keeping the earliest work in each group (the earliest row can never match its own condition, so it survives automatically, no separate keep-rule needed). - Live recordings are excluded from the Works dataset entirely (same reasoning as compilations/unofficial: a performance of existing material, not a new stylistic work). No reliable Discogs tag exists for this (format_descriptions and style both checked, neither carries a "Live" value), so detection is title-only (regex \blive\b, case-insensitive). Checked the word following "live" in matching titles: dominated by "at"/"in"/"from"/end-of-title/parenthetical patterns consistent with real live albums, accepted residual noise in both directions (idiom false positives like "Live To Tell", untitled-as-live false negatives). 92,531 rows removed, works_df now 6,017,182 rows.
- "Greatest Hits" works that slipped through the earlier is_compilation exclusion (Discogs did not tag them Compilation in format_descriptions) are removed by title match ("greatest hits", case-insensitive). Small population (1,622 rows, 0.027%), same reasoning as the Live exclusion: a compilation of previously released material, not new creative work. works_df now 6,015,560 rows.
- Other clear compilation-type titles that also slipped through is_compilation ("best of", "anthology", "box set", "discography", "b-sides"/"bsides", "rarities") are removed the same way. Sample of 20 confirmed overwhelmingly real compilations, one accepted edge case ("Best Of Me", could be an original song title, same order of noise as Live/reissue). 6,622 rows removed, works_df now 6,008,938 rows.
- "Remixes"-titled works (regex remixes|remix album) are removed: a package of mostly third-party remixes of an already-existing track, credited back to the original artist, same reasoning as reissue (repackaging, not a new creative step). Sample of 20 was uniformly clean, no ambiguous originals. 24,917 rows removed, works_df now 5,984,021 rows.
- "Tribute"-titled works (regex \btribute\b) are removed: overwhelmingly "Tribute To [other artist]", covering/performing someone else's material as homage, not the credited artist's own new work. A handful of standalone matches ("Tribute", "My Tribute") don't name a target and could be original compositions, accepted as the same order of noise as other patterns. 5,394 rows removed, works_df now 5,978,627 rows.

- Fuzzy-dedup implemented in src/works.py (remove_fuzzy_duplicate_titles): within each artist_id, using window-10 alphabetical blocking, pairs scoring >=90 on rapidfuzz token_sort_ratio are collapsed unless a number/roman-numeral guard applies. The guard strips embedded digit runs from every token (so "mix4" vs "mix7", "S01E03" vs "S01E05" are also caught, not just space-separated numbers like "Vol. 2" vs "Vol. 3") and skips the pair if titles are only different by that numeric content. Threshold chosen because a stricter 95+ cutoff would have missed known true duplicates in Bing Crosby's catalog (scores 89.5 and 91.4). Tie-break on a collapsed pair keeps the earlier year (missing year treated as latest), ties broken by smaller work_id. Verified at full scale against works_checkpoint.parquet (6,114,471 rows): removed 21,846 rows (0.36%), works_df now 6,092,625 rows. Two accepted known limitations: (1) "Vol."/"Volume" abbreviation mismatches aren't caught by the guard, so a small number of genuine volume-series pairs may still collide (full-dataset band sampling showed minimal residual noise after the guard, so this looks rare); (2) shared-template titles with a non-numeric varying word (e.g. a medley "X / Y" title where Y differs, such as "White Christmas / The First Nowell" vs "...The First Snowfall") can produce false positives, spot-checked at 1 of 11 in the Bing Crosby test. - Genre disparity in the recovery above is non-uniform and NOT resolved by it: a read-only per-genre breakdown found recovery rate correlates strongly negatively with each genre's original missing-year rate (Spearman r=-0.809, p=0.0026, n=11 genres), meaning genres that already had the easiest time (Rock 53.8% recovery, Electronic 47.9%, Blues 50.1%) recover at roughly 50%, while the hardest-hit genres (Latin 32.8%, Folk/World/&Country 30.2%, Reggae 38.9%) recover at the lowest rates. - Missing-year artist dropout: do not drop artists/works with missing year. Rationale (2026-10-08): dropping the 173,938 artists (16.8% of 1,034,756) who have missing year would remove 1,995,677 works (33.6% of the 5,949,217 total), with a material genre/style skew in the dropped population (affected artists over-indexed in high-missing-rate genres: Latin 25.7%→36.6%, Folk/World/&Country 21.5%→37.2%, Reggae 23.0%→31.1%, Punk 2.6%→18.8%, Folk style 11.7%→29.5%; under-indexed in low-missing-rate genres: Electronic 1.5%→11.3%, Tech House 0.4%→4.8%; range of differences +5% to +17.8pp). Not a neutral trim of noise, but a selective removal of entire categories. Approach: keep missing-year works; add an explicit year_missing boolean feature; use a model family that splits on missingness natively (LightGBM/HistGradientBoostingClassifier), not sklearn's default RandomForest. Supporting numbers: 173,938/1,034,756 artists affected; of those, 39,706 fully missing (all works undated), 134,232 partially missing (mixed); 510,364 of their 1,995,677 works missing year (25.6%); genre/style distribution skew documented in the pasted analysis from notebooks/02_eda_visualization.ipynb re-run 2026-10-08 (before finalizing train/test split, revisit this decision if model performance on missing-year works drifts significantly at modeling stage).
- WHEN target is measured at year level, snapshots stay one per dated work (2026-10-10, src/snapshots.py): next_1y/next_2y use the gap to the artist's next year with activity after the snapshot's own year (always >= 1), not the next individual Work. Reason: 63% of dates are year-only, and with the next-Work definition 48.2% of snapshots (56.2% with 3+ prior works) had the next Work in the same year (gap 0), forcing next_1y/next_2y positive (76.2%/85.9% base rates). Re-validated on the full checkpoint (5,438,765 snapshots, 510,357 undated and 95 no-artist works dropped): next_1y 51.8%, next_2y 64.1% (62.4%/74.0% with 3+ prior works), 66.1% of next active years fall exactly 1 year later, and an independent recompute of both targets matches exactly. Added works_this_year (whole-year count, so it includes later same-year works), work_source/genres/year_missing pass-through, a deterministic within-year order (see the so-far entry), and 2025 as the last complete year for censoring (the 2026-09-01 dump makes 2026 partial). Known effect: next_1y rises with works_this_year (29.3% for 1 work, 86.3% for 5+), so productivity is a strong WHEN signal.

- Within-year productivity features split into model-safe and descriptive-only (2026-10-10, src/snapshots.py): works_so_far_this_year (works up to and including this one in the artist/year/work_id sort order), albums_so_far_this_year and singles_so_far_this_year are the modeling-facing features. works_this_year, albums_this_year and singles_this_year count the whole year, so they include same-year works that come after the snapshot's work and leak future information within the year; they stay in the output for EDA only and are marked in the docstring as not safe as model inputs. Re-check on the full checkpoint (5,438,765 snapshots), next_1y by works count: 1 work 38.7% (so far) vs 29.3% (whole year), 2 works 61.2% vs 52.3%, 3-4 74.3% vs 67.0%, 5-10 87.2% vs 83.6%, 11+ 92.4% vs 92.2%; Spearman with next_1y 0.351 (so far) vs 0.445 (whole year). On first-of-year rows only (all have works_so_far = 1, so the feature carries no information there), the whole-year count alone still separates next_1y from 29.3% to 92.0%, which is the leaked part: about a fifth of the correlation (0.445 to 0.351) disappears, and the rest of the signal (38.7% to 92.4% across the buckets) is genuine within-year productivity known at the snapshot. Caveat: the within-year order is not real chronology (the data only knows the year), so the so-far features are a proxy, and the strictly leak-free cutoff is the year. The first version ordered same-year works by work_id, which put every master before every synthetic work (synthetic ids carry the 10_000_000_000 offset); replaced the same day by a seeded pseudo-random order (hash of work_id plus WITHIN_YEAR_ORDER_SEED = 1, reproducible and independent of input row order). Re-check: Spearman of works_so_far_this_year with next_1y unchanged at 0.351 and the bucket rates unchanged to 3 decimals (38.7%, 61.2%, 74.3%, 87.2%, 92.4%), so the remaining signal is genuine productivity, not a source artifact; in the 358,052 artist-years that mix masters and synthetic works, the first slot is now synthetic 52.5% of the time (54.3% of those works are synthetic), was 0% by construction. The same order drives history_count, years_since_last and the next-work comparison in new_style.

## Hypothesis Testing

**Hypothesis 1**: Does an artist's new-style introduction rate change with how many prior works they have? H0: new_style and prior_work_bucket are independent. H1: they are dependent. Caveat: transitions from the same artist are not fully independent, so the p-value is overstated; the rate gap is the stronger evidence.

**Method**: Chi-square test of independence, computed on 3,254,604 eligible transitions (min_previous_works=3). Transitions bucketed by prior-work count into five groups: 3-5 prior works, 6-10, 11-20, 21-50, 51+.

**Results** (notebooks/02_eda_visualization.ipynb cell hypothesis testing, executed 2026-10-08):
- 3-5 prior works: 927,574 transitions, 26.88% introduce new style
- 6-10 prior works: 729,148 transitions, 20.64% introduce new style
- 11-20 prior works: 632,029 transitions, 15.72% introduce new style
- 21-50 prior works: 576,937 transitions, 11.18% introduce new style
- 51+ prior works: 388,916 transitions, 6.20% introduce new style

**Chi-square test**: chi-square = 109,737.05, p-value < 0.000001, degrees of freedom = 4. **Result: REJECT null hypothesis** (p < 0.05).

**Interpretation**: The new-style introduction rate varies significantly by prior-work bucket, with a strong monotonic decline as artists accumulate more prior works. This is not noise; artist history length is a real signal that predicts stylistic consistency. The effect is large: a 4x difference between the lowest (6.20%) and highest (26.88%) buckets.
