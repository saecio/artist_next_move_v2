## Demo artist selection: eligibility rule (17-10-2026)

Context: the Time Machine demo needs well-known artists across genres, chosen before any score is viewed.

Decision: an artist is a candidate if at least one of these holds for ceremony years 2019 to 2026:
- a Grammy Album of the Year nomination;
- a Primetime Emmy nomination in Outstanding Variety Special (Live), Outstanding Variety Special (Pre-Recorded), Outstanding Music Direction or Outstanding Original Music and Lyrics.
Popularity filler chosen by the author is also admitted, backed where possible by Billboard year-end 2022, the Billboard Latin Music Awards 2019 to 2025, Pitchfork 2022 or the Latin Grammy. One nomination is enough, there is no minimum count.

Limitation: selecting by nominations favours artists whose nominated work coincides with a stylistic change. The demo list is therefore not a random sample and says nothing about global performance. Global metrics come only from the holdout.

Sources (accessed 17-10-2026): en.wikipedia.org pages for the Grammy Album of the Year and for the four Emmy categories above; grammy.com category pages; billboard.com year-end and Latin charts.

## Demo: timeline rule per artist and year, and baseline label (17-10-2026)

Decision: the demo timeline shows one row per artist and year. Each artist-year is represented by its last Work in the within-year order of the WHEN snapshots table (src/snapshots.py: sorted by artist_id, year, within_year_rank, work_id, where within_year_rank is a hash of work_id plus a seed from config.yaml), that is, the Work whose works_so_far_this_year equals works_this_year, shown with its own score and its own label. ## Demo: live scoring with the final models (17-10-2026)

Decision: the demo app scores live with the final models: WHEN XGBoost final for next_1y and next_2y (FINAL_XGB_PARAMS in src/models.py, entries "XGBoost tuning results, three phases" and "FINAL holdout evaluation"), WHAT stage 0 (entries "WHAT XGBoost adoption evaluation: stage 0 kept" and "WHAT final holdout evaluation of stage 0"), and, for the style forecast, the Random Forest with the locked configuration (the one adopted in the entry "Style trend forecast, adoption and pass rule results, final test, 2024 projection and output procedure") and repeat_last (no model needed; it stays in the files and in the controls and is not displayed in the app, see entry "Demo: tab 7 shows the Random Forest (19-10-2026)"). The demo includes only final models: the models that were only comparison models in the style forecast (Bayesian Ridge and XGBoost) are not scored in the app, and their results are shown only from the saved result files, in the method tab. Hyperparameters and seeds are those logged in those entries and in "Style trend forecast, adoption and pass rule results, final test, 2024 projection and output procedure", and are not restated here.
Refit: the WHEN and WHAT models are refitted once with the logged hyperparameters and seed on the same training rows as their holdout evaluations (years before 2018 only, as logged in those entries) and saved in a small folder. No holdout evaluation is repeated and no new holdout metric is computed. The metrics shown in the app are the ones already saved in results/holdout_ci95, results/holdout_what and results/style_forecast. The final WHEN and WHAT models were not saved by the scripts that produced the holdout results, which is why they are refitted and checked against the stored scores.
Control: before saving or using any model, live scores must reproduce the stored ones to 4 decimals: results/demo/when_demo_scores.csv for WHEN and results/holdout_what/row_scores.csv for WHAT (stage0_score), on the rows of the demo artists, and all rows of results/style_forecast/predictions.csv for the Random Forest and repeat_last (2018 to 2022 and 2024), growth predictions and ranges. The step stops if any comparison fails.
Style forecast scope: the app scores the style forecast live for every target year of predictions.csv (2018 to 2022 and 2024; tab 7 also scores 2023 live from an extra model, entry "Demo: live style forecast for 2023 and later years (19-10-2026)"). For each target year T of 2018 to 2022, one Random Forest is refitted with the locked configuration and seed (LOCKED_RF_PARAMS in src/style_forecast.py; entry "Style trend forecast, adoption and pass rule results, final test, 2024 projection and output procedure", which also fixes the walk-forward procedure) on the training rows of that target year (origins at most T - 2, labels known), following the walk_forward procedure of scripts/run_style_forecast_test.py and predicting the styles eligible at origin T - 1. The 2024 rows use the existing Random Forest bundle of results/style_forecast/models. repeat_last needs no model. The bundles in results/style_forecast/models stay untouched and are not used by the app. The per-year models are saved in a small folder together with what the ranges need. From count_table and count_bounds in src/style_forecast.py, the range of a row is the expected Works multiplied by exp of the 10th and 90th percentile of the log residuals log(actual / expected) of earlier target years, pooled (all folds scored before T for 2018 to 2022; every scored fold for 2024), where actual is c_next clipped at 1 and expected is base x exp(prediction) for the Random Forest and c_t clipped at 1 for repeat_last. These residuals come from the Random Forest fits of the development years, which are not saved. Limitation: these residuals cannot be recomputed from the saved models alone, so the two percentiles per target year and method are computed once by the refit step and stored as a small table, and the control checks that the ranges built from that table match predictions.csv. Size cap: the folder of per-year models must stay below 150 MB in total (compressed joblib); if it does not, the step stops and reports instead of dropping anything silently.
Inputs: small files only. For WHEN and WHAT, one file with the feature rows of the demo artists (the 40 fixed ids in app/demo_config.yaml), built from the existing snapshots and artist-year tables. For the style forecast, the style-by-origin rows of every origin needed by the per-year models and by the 2024 projection (not only origin 2023), built by build_panel in src/style_forecast.py and saved as one small file. The small Works file of the demo artists carries works_so_far_this_year and works_this_year. The app never reads snapshots.parquet or any large file and does not compute features from raw data.
Limitation: live scoring of WHEN and WHAT works only for the 40 demo artists and the 21 artists of pool B (entry "Demo pool B: final list (19-10-2026)"), not for arbitrary artists. The range of target years is not the same in every tab (WHAT 2018 to the last data year for the artist-years with enough earlier Works, see the entry "Demo: WHAT live assets, 2018 to the last data year (19-10-2026)"; WHEN 2018 to 2023 with 2024 to 2026 as projection; style 2018 to 2024 with 2023 and 2024 flagged and no forecast for 2025 and 2026), and the app must label it on screen. Baselines (for example baseline (c) in WHAT) are not models; the demo screens show only final models, and baselines appear in the method and results tabs, labelled as baselines.

## Demo: tab 6 flow and scoring (19-10-2026)

Decision: the question on screen is "Will the artist release something next year?" (WHEN next_1y). The word is release (entry "Text rules for the audience (20-10-2026)"), because the target counts any next release: the year-level activity of the artist, defined in the entry "WHEN target is measured at year level, snapshots stay one per dated work (10-10-2026)". The user prediction is optional: a checkbox "My prediction" (off by default, kept per artist and changeable between years) adds a yes or no question and a lock button before step A. Step A shows the model result, step B the reality (the stored label). A "Go to {year}" button moves to the next playable year (a year with a Work and a score, or a year without a Work that has a year-end score, see below); in this mode (one artist) there is no year selector, and the timeline is the window of the entry "Demo: tab 6 mode 1 layout: compact bar, windowed timeline, two-column panel (20-10-2026)", with the future years as "?". Tab 6 has a second mode, several artists in one year (entry "Demo: tab 6 mode 2, several artists in one year (19-10-2026)"), which opens first and has a year selector. Each year card draws up to poc.titles_per_card (3) titles from results/demo/works_demo.csv, with a "+N more" line when the year has more, and a short line with the count of hidden re-recordings (entry "Demo: re-recordings (17-10-2026)"); the score, verdict and label of a year are those of its representative Work.
Scoring: the scoreboard is model vs reality, with an optional user column (user vs reality and user vs model) when the checkbox is on; years already played keep the columns they had. The model says yes when its live score is at least 0.5, the default threshold of the entry "FINAL holdout evaluation (13-10-2026)" (precision and recall of next_1y reported at 0.5, a convention and not a tuned operating point). The WHEN side panel is the cascade of mode 2, built by the same functions (multi_rows of src/demo_multi.py, side_panel_html of src/poc_view.py): the "Next year" pill and, only when the next-year verdict or the 2-year verdict is No, an arrow and the "Within {n} years" pill; after the reveal the reality cascade follows in the same structure, with the release title in the Yes cells (entry "Demo: release title of the reality cells (20-10-2026)"). The mode 1 panel is two question blocks, Timing and Style, with the wording of the entry "Tab 6 panel wording, independent Timing and Style questions, new styles in the WHAT reality (20-10-2026)"; the Timing reality is "Reality: Yes" or "Reality: No" with the release title and year on the line below (rule B). The user prediction applies only to the next-year question, and the scoreboard counts only the next-year result, by the stored counted_1y flag (a result shown for reference only is not counted); the 2-year result is never scored. WHAT is shown below WHEN in the right panel, under the title "Style of the next release" (game.what_title), which answers a different question than WHEN: the style of the next release, whenever it comes. Step A: a verdict pill, Likely when the WHAT score is at or above score_cutoff and Not likely otherwise (poc.texts.likely and not_likely, the pills of mode 2). The cutoff is the one of the top 10% rule already registered (rows with a score at or above the 90th percentile of the holdout scores; entries "WHAT threshold-metric report (14-10-2026)" and "WHAT final holdout evaluation of stage 0 (14-10-2026)"), not a new threshold, and it is read at load time from results/holdout_what/top10_lift.csv (row stage 0: score_cutoff 0.4943, precision_top 0.5980, holdout_positive_rate 0.3128). The reliability lines sit in the closed "How to read this" expander. For a Yes verdict the line reads "In past tests, about {x} in 10 artists in this group did bring a new style, against about {y} in 10 for any artist.", with x and y the precision_top and the holdout_positive_rate of that row rounded to the nearest integer out of 10 (6 and 3 with those figures). For a No verdict the line reads "In past tests, about {z} in 10 artists outside this group also brought a new style, almost the same as for any artist ({y} in 10).", with z the positive rate of the holdout rows whose stage0_score is below score_cutoff in results/holdout_what/row_scores.csv, rounded out of 10 (128,905 rows below the cutoff, 36,240 positives, rate 0.2811, so z is 3 against y 3); the words "almost the same" are used only when the rounded z and y differ by at most one, otherwise the neutral wording "compared with {y} in 10 for any artist" is used, and with the real values the first wording applies. The scores are not on screen: each is the tooltip of its prediction word, never a percentage. The audience is not familiar with ML, so no calibration warning is shown (calibration checked on 21-10-2026, see docs/decisions_core.md entry "Calibration check on the final holdout (21-10-2026)"); the verdict is a ranking statement about the top 10% group and not a probability.
